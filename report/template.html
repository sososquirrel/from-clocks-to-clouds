Koopman VAE · one model, five views

Minimal Clock Koopman

A clock image is compressed into four numbers, and one 4×4 matrix K moves those numbers forward in time. This page follows that single model from the plain VAE to its Koopman spectrum and modes.

data      64→32 px clock images, hour and minute hands independent, 1 step = 1 minute (minute +6°, hour +0.5°)
stage 1   VAE, latent d = 4:   loss = reconstruction + KL
stage 2   + Koopman, K = 4×4 (starts at the identity), 12,000 iterations, horizon up to 36 minutes:
          loss = reconstruction + KL + 125·‖Kᵏμ₀ − μₖ‖²/Var(μ) + BCE(decode(Kᵏμ₀), xₖ)

KL against Koopman: the trade-off

The two terms want different latents. The KL pulls codes towards 0 and wants wide blobs; the Koopman loss wants every code exactly on its circle, with a precise phase, so that Kᵗ predicts it. Each panel shows one quantity against β (dots: seeds).

What the KL does here

It does not change what Koopman finds: every β learns the same two modes, (1, 0) at 0.5° and (1, 1) at 6.5° per minute. It shapes the latent: without KL the encoder makes tiny blobs and stretches the latent freely, so nearby codes can decode to very different clocks. The KL fixes the scale and makes the decoder smooth around every code, which forgives the small errors Kᵗ accumulates over long rollouts. The effect is a threshold, not a slope: for β ≤ 0.5 the 6-hour error stays near the autoencoder's (about 10 ×10⁻³, with 6–7% of clocks failing completely), while β ≥ 1 cuts it to about 4 ×10⁻³ with no failures (same result on two seeds for β = 0 and β = 1). Too much KL blurs the images. Random samples from N(0, I) are poor for every β: the clocks form a torus, which a Gaussian prior does not match.

How to choose β in practice

  1. Judge β by long-horizon prediction on held-out data, not by reconstruction (reconstruction always prefers β = 0). Here: β = 1 or 2.
  2. Sweep β on a log scale with the rest of the recipe fixed.
  3. Watch the KL when the Koopman loss switches on: a rise means Koopman is pulling codes away from the prior.
  4. Keep the latent loss scale-free (divided by Var(μ)), so the KL's control of the scale does not change the weight of the Koopman term.
2 · Training

The latent space while the model learns

Final modes applies the α and β recipes of the final model (box below) to the 4 numbers stored at each checkpoint: left panel α(z), right panel β(z)·conj(α(z)), whose angles are the hour and minute hands once training is done. Early on the recipes do not fit yet, so the points are scattered; they fold onto circles as the encoder converges.

The two coordinates α and β

The model stores each clock as 4 numbers z = (z₁, z₂, z₃, z₄). The trained K has two rotating directions, and reading z along each one gives a point in a plane: two weighted sums of z₁…z₄, one for x and one for y. These two points are α and β.

z ∈ ℝ⁴ : latent code of a clock (encoder mean)        K ∈ ℝ⁴ˣ⁴ : learned Koopman matrix

K = V Λ V⁻¹,   Λ = diag(λ_α, λ̄_α, λ_β, λ̄_β),   λ_α = e^{iω_α}, ω_α = 0.504°,   λ_β = e^{iω_β}, ω_β = 6.503°
w_α, w_β ∈ ℂ⁴ : rows of V⁻¹ (left eigenvectors)      w_αᵀ K = λ_α w_αᵀ,   w_βᵀ K = λ_β w_βᵀ

α(z) = c_α · w_αᵀ z ∈ ℂ          β(z) = c_β · w_βᵀ z ∈ ℂ
c_α, c_β ∈ ℂ : fixed constants (one rotation + one scale) so that arg α ≈ θ_h, arg β ≈ θ_h + θ_m, median |·| = 1

Koopman eigenfunctions:   α(K z) = λ_α α(z)   ⇒   α(z_{t+1}) = e^{iω_α} α(z_t)
                          β(K z) = λ_β β(z)   ⇒   β(z_{t+1}) = e^{iω_β} β(z_t)      (|λ| = 1: radius unchanged)

Hands:   arg α(z) = θ_h,   arg β(z) = θ_h + θ_m   ⇒   θ_m = arg β(z) − arg α(z) = arg( β(z) · conj α(z) )
Plots:   x = Re α(z), y = Im α(z)   (likewise for β and β · conj α)

Written out with the trained numbers, w = c · (row of V⁻¹), real and imaginary parts:

Read the angle of each point, measured from the x axis, counter-clockwise. The angle of α(z) is the hour-hand angle θ_h. The angle of β(z) is θ_h + θ_m (hour plus minute). Each step of time (one minute), K turns α by 0.504° and β by 6.503°. The minute hand is the difference: θ_m = angle(β) − angle(α), which is the angle of β·conj(α).

4 · Spectrum

Two rotations, nothing else

K has four eigenvalues: two conjugate pairs, both on the unit circle (|λ| = 1, nothing decays). Each pair is a rotation of a 2-D plane of the latent, at a fixed angle per minute.

What the model learned (α and β, defined in section 2)


      
(1, 1) means the mode turns with θ_h + θ_m. Both modes rotate at constant speed and together they identify every clock, so reconstruction and prediction are exact.
